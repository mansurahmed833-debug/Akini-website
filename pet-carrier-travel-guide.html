Choosing a Pet Carrier for Safer, Calmer Journeys

A carrier should be secure, ventilated and appropriate for the journey. A short trip to the vet and a longer train or car journey may call for different practical features.

Match the carrier to the animal

Your pet should be able to stand naturally where the design allows, turn around and lie down comfortably. Check the maker’s size and weight guidance, and measure your pet rather than relying on breed labels. A carrier that is too small can be uncomfortable; one that is too large may allow excessive movement during transport.

Check construction and ventilation

Inspect the door, locking points, seams and handle before use. Ventilation openings should remain unobstructed. For nervous animals, a stable structure and a familiar washable blanket may help. Soft-sided carriers can be easier to store, while rigid carriers may be more suitable for some forms of transport; confirm the rules of the airline or transport operator before travelling.

Make the carrier familiar

Leave it open at home with bedding or treats so your pet can explore it voluntarily. Build up to short, calm practice sessions. Never force an animal into a carrier as a routine training method. Secure the carrier in a vehicle according to the vehicle and carrier instructions, and never leave an animal unattended in a hot car.

Travel checklist

Bring water, waste bags or absorbent liners, familiar bedding and any essential documents. For international journeys, check current animal identification, vaccination and transport requirements with a veterinarian and the relevant authorities well in advance.
