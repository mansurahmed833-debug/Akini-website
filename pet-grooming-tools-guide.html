Pets

A Practical Guide to Everyday Pet Grooming Tools

Grooming at home can support comfort and help you notice changes in your pet’s coat or skin. The right tools depend on coat type, temperament and the care your veterinarian or groomer recommends.

Start with the coat type

Short, smooth coats may need only a soft brush or grooming mitt. Longer or dense coats may need a comb that reaches through the coat without pulling. Curly or double coats can require more specialised techniques. If you are unsure, ask a qualified groomer to demonstrate the correct tool and method for your pet.

Choose gentle, suitable tools

Look for rounded or protected tips where appropriate, a comfortable handle and a tool that is easy to clean. Avoid cutting mats close to the skin with scissors: skin can be caught in the mat. Severe tangles, painful areas or skin changes are best handled by a professional or veterinarian.

Make grooming a positive routine

Begin with short sessions and reward calm behaviour. Brush in the direction that is comfortable for your pet and stop if they show stress or discomfort. Check ears, paws and nails gently, but do not insert tools into the ear canal or cut nails beyond the safe area. Ask a veterinarian to show you if you are uncertain.

Keep tools clean

Remove loose hair after each session and wash tools according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Dry them fully before storing. Replace tools with bent teeth, sharp edges or damaged handles.

AKINI takeaway

Buy for your pet’s actual coat and comfort, not for a large grooming kit. A few appropriate tools used gently are often more useful than many unsuitable ones.
