How to Choose a Cat Litter Box and Set It Up Well

A litter box is a daily-use essential, so its size, access and location can make a real difference to household routines. The best option is one your cat can enter comfortably and use without feeling trapped.

Get the size and entry right

Your cat should be able to turn around, dig and cover waste without squeezing into a corner. Larger cats often need a larger tray. Kittens, older cats and cats with mobility limitations may find a low entry easier. Covered boxes can contain some scatter, but some cats dislike enclosed spaces; observe your cat rather than assuming one style is best.

Choose a practical location

Place the tray somewhere quiet and accessible, away from food and water bowls and noisy appliances. Avoid locations where a door can block access. In multi-level homes, consider whether your cat can reach a tray on each level, especially if mobility is an issue.

Plan cleaning and litter compatibility

Scoop waste regularly and wash the tray with mild, unscented products. Strong fragrances can be unpleasant to cats. If changing litter type, mix the new material gradually rather than switching abruptly. Follow the litter manufacturer’s disposal guidance and local waste rules; do not flush litter unless the packaging and local guidance explicitly allow it.

How many trays?

A commonly used starting guideline is one tray per cat plus one extra, placed in separate accessible locations. The right setup can vary with the home and the cats’ relationships. If a cat suddenly stops using the tray, consult a veterinarian to rule out a health problem rather than treating it only as a behaviour issue.

AKINI takeaway

Prioritise generous space, easy access, a quiet location and a cleaning routine your household can maintain.
